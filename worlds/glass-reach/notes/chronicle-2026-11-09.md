# The Glass Reach, the first hosted game (2026-11-09)
Written from the server's own event log of the hosted world, with the game master's log beside it for the hours of the clock.

## RUHL

The dry came on him in the yard before the sun was well clear of the wall.

He knew ordinary thirst. This was not that. It began as a thickness on the tongue, and within a few breaths the throat had closed like a fist, and every breath after was worse than the one before, as if the day had been wound wrong and was running down too fast.

He had the ring scratched in the sand, the gate shut, the inner door with its cistern and its writ and nine days of a new reeve behind it. From none of them came anyone. He stood as long as a captain stands, and then he knelt, and then the sand had him.

The worst of it was that it did not stop. A man goes down and the thing that took him should be done with him. This went on past the bottom, a point at a time, every minute, into a dark he had no name for, while nobody came through the gate.

## CORIN

The yard was empty except for the Captain, and the Captain was on his face by the ring.

Corin knelt beside him. "Captain, it's Vass. You're dry as the Flats. Where can I get water to you, quickest?" What came back was a drill-yard lesson. "Walk the Flats loud and the sand comes up and eats you. The Keth walk it like they are dancing. Learn that or stay off the glass."

There was no skin on him. Corin went in to the cistern, a stone tank under the floor with a crack you could put two fingers in, and drank from his hands. The reeve stood by the tank with the look of a man not yet awake. "Reeve Varrick. Corin Vass, one of Ruhl's nine. The Captain sent me to be your man. But the Captain's down in the yard, dry as bone. Have you a waterskin, sir? I'll fill it at the cistern and get it to him."

In the corner a clerk with ink on two fingers smiled with the lower half of his face. "Guild business, Reeve. Dull as dust." Then the reeve turned. "Hey, Corin! Glad you could make it! We have water to get back to this Hold!"

The skin did not come. "My lord, your goatskin, if you please. Hand it here and I'll fill it from the cistern and get it to the Captain. He's lying in the yard with nothing in him. Every minute out there costs him."

When that went nowhere he went down the white road to the Carriers' Landing and asked the woman with the ledger for a skin. "No glass, no water; the Guild does not run a well."

He came back to find the reeve in the yard with a filled skin, and the yard doing to the reeve what it had done to the Captain. It did it to Corin too: a few minutes in that sun and his own throat went, and he went in and drank, nine draws, and came out, and watched the reeve go grey with the full skin in his fist, and fold.

"My lord. The skin, now. Give it to me, or kneel and put it to his mouth yourself. He won't see noon without it, and neither will the wall."

Nobody answered. Corin went in and out, eight draws, seven. Once he saw the reeve get the skin to his own mouth down in the sand, and swallow, and not rise. Then the reeve crawled on his belly back through the inner door. Corin took the Captain's sword out of the sand, and the reeve's blade and ward off the reeve, and kept them.

Then a sheet appeared, nailed to the frame of the cistern with the ink still wet, in a hand nobody in the Hold writes. And the reeve stood up. Out in the yard the Captain was on his feet, square and grey, as if he had never been down. Corin put the skin in the reeve's hand, and when he looked into the tank the water stood at twelve draws, as though the morning had been struck out and begun over.

## ANSEL

The sheet by the cistern said the Reach had run a dry day and that it was mended. He read it twice. Corin was at his elbow with the skin, and the clerk was in his corner.

Ansel raised two fingers at the clerk. "Go to hell, Hollis."

"Corin. Let's go out onto the road and talk to Ossa."

Ossa gave him the words she had given Corin. "Okay, Ossa. Then we can go get you the shard." Glass underfoot, warm already, every step ringing. He worked a shard out of the red vein and the dune slid for no reason he could see, and he crept back on the quiet step with it against his chest. "Corin, I have the shard. Come back to the road, and come quiet, the sand is listening."

"Fair glass. A full skin, and no questions where you dug it." He gave the Guild's skin to Corin and asked Ossa about the clerk in his Hold. "Hollis is no clerk of mine. The paper was Dreyer's. I took the glass and said nothing, and now I have said it." She tapped the ledger. "Water in, glass out. Talk to me in those two words and we understand each other."

A second shard, a second skin. By then the glare came up off the glass until there was no ground, only light.

"Corin, we should go up to the ridge. Red Ridge, past the Flats. Are you with me?"

From the ridge the Reach was a sheet of black glass with red dunes walking across it, and the Hold a thumbprint to the south. Two Keth sat in the shade of the rock, a woman and a young man with a long glass that did not leave the Hold.

He offered the woman the full skin, freely, from the reeve of Scar Gate. "Hm." He offered again. "Hm." He put it into her hands anyway and said the rest: the cistern cracked and failing, nine men dry within days, the reeve's peace and open trade for water, none of Dreyer's poison ever again, for a way to the Deepwell until the cistern was mended. She did not answer in words. The skin stayed in her hands, which was an answer.

Behind him his man had begun to talk to the one with the glass.

## SEFA

She had gone down on this rock in the morning, as Nico had, and had come back from it with a full skin at her side and no memory of filling it. Neither spoke of it.

Then the two from the Hold came up, the reeve soft-footed and the guard not. "Greetings Nico, and Sefa, I am Corin and my Companion Reeve... Well my guiide really. What is this place ?" She let the rock answer him. The reeve put his skin in her hands and said what he wanted with the Flats, water and not glass, and gave his word on it.

The guard tried Nico. "You have no respect. No words for  us ?" The glass did not move. "If you choose not to speak. You might find yourself at the end of my blade"

"Reeve. The skin is heavy and it is yours, and I have been quiet too long over it. You said what you want with the Flats: water, not glass. That I can carry to Taan." And to the guard: "Corin Vass. You are wounded, dry, and a long way from your nine. The end of your blade would not get you water up here."

"You are rude... prepare to die"

He drew the reeve's own blade and cut at her, and she was not where the cut went. "Rude is not speaking. Dying is drawing on a Keth on Keth rock with a wound in you and two of us watching. Pick which you want to be. I am still offering water." Then, to the man whose word she held: "Reeve. Your man. Yours to leash, or the way down closes before it opens."

The reeve said nothing. He sat in the shade with his eyes on his Hold.

"Sorry Reeve, asks for mercy for you"

"Good. That is the first careful thing you have said. Keep it sheathed and drink when we get down, not before."

The guard sat. It was Nico who broke first, the glass lowered a finger's width: he had watched the guard draw on Sefa and watched him put it away, and the second thing was what he would tell Taan. Then the glass went back. "Hold is quiet. Gate shut. One thread of smoke from the yard, cookfire, not forge."

## DRAM

"Twelve draws, Carrier. I counted them before you did. The cistern is the Hold's and the crack in it is the Hold's. You stand by it a long time for a man whose business is dull as dust."

So the Captain had marked him. Hollis had read the sheet by the cistern and understood that the thirst he had been sent to keep sharp was a lever now and not a blade. Stand by the water less, then. Stand by the ring.

He came out into the glare with his book under his arm. "Yard's no place for ink, Carrier. This is the ring. Step into it and the old law has you, same as any man." He made a mark in the book and stayed in the shade.

Past noon, the hour he had been given arrived without asking him. He walked into the ring, let the coat fall, drew, and asked the yard for the reeve by name. "Ansel Varrick. Reeve of Scar Gate by the Crown's writ. The yard is a ring by old law, and I have read the law in your own book. Any man may call. Any reeve must answer." And: "Come out into the glare, Reeve, and answer, or let the Hold hear what a writ is worth when the man who holds it stays indoors."

Nobody came out. After a while he looked into the Hold himself. "His Hold is empty, Captain. I looked. The writ is nailed up in there and the man is not under it."

The Captain heard him out, and the six on the wall heard him too. He would not answer a call made to another; he held the ring, and in it nobody worked a man over from behind and nobody touched the gate or the cistern. Until the reeve walked back in, point out of the sand.

Hollis lifted the point as he was told. "Six men on the wall. Good. Six is a number I can write down." He settled his weight. "I am good at waiting. Ask anyone at the Landing; they will tell you it is the only thing I am good at."

"Wait, then. Wait where I can see you."

## NICO

"Nico you talk i hear ?"

"When there is something to say." The glass did not move. "Sit in the shade, guard. The sun does not care who you are."

The guard wanted to know when they would attack. The glass came down for that. "Attack who? Nobody here is attacking. Your reeve gave his word and Sefa gave the path."

Far down in the Hold's yard something caught the sun the way only bare steel does, and held it, and small shapes drifted toward the ring scratched in the sand. He kept it for Taan.

The reeve came back from wherever he had been behind his eyes and leashed his man. "Sefa. My man is leashed, and my word stands: sheathed blades, your steps, no talking on the sand. The word is given. Walk us down to Taan." Sefa took it. "The word is heard, Reeve, and I keep it." And to Nico, low: "Hold the rock. Tell no one where we went."

She went down the quiet way at the back of the ridge, and the reeve did not follow. Nico went down after her, "Follow in my steps and set your foot where I set mine," and the reeve did not follow him either. Sefa came back up. "You did not come, Reeve. The rock hears the word and the feet have to follow it." She went down a second time, and still the reeve stood on the rock and told his man to hold in the shade.

There was nothing to find at the back of the ridge: a lip of worn stone, two sets of soft prints going one way, cool air out of the dark. Nobody found it. It was shown. Nico put his voice up the shaft. "Reeve. Sefa and Taan are at the water. Come down. The broken step, behind me, nobody talking."

The reeve came down alone. "Quiet step, reeve. Good. Taan is there, and the water is behind her. Whatever you ask here, you ask her, not me. I only watch."

## TAAN

Sefa presented him: come down quiet and on his own feet, his word hers to give the Elder now.

"Elder Taan. Ansel Varrick, reeve of Scar Gate by the Crown's writ, nine days in it. I came down on my own feet, blade sheathed, in Sefa's steps, because your water is the only honest water in this reach and my Hold's cistern is cracked. I did not come to ask you nothing."

"Four of mine died at the Low Well, two of them children. The Hold did that, whatever name it wore. I do not forget it. I may set it down."

He offered terms, a long list, and she did not take them, and said the same words again.

"This water was here before the Hold and will be here after. Dreyer wanted it and put poison in the Low Well. You want it too. Tell me why you are not Dreyer, in terms I can carry." He offered again and fell short again, and asked for her own terms. "Terms are not a wish. Say what the Hold gives and what it stays away from, and we will see if it weighs anything."

So he said what the Hold gave: the Low Well named a Keth grave at the Hold's own gate, its four dead on his name; Dreyer's man handed to Keth judgment; iron, salt and goats for every measure of water, the Keth naming the measure. And what it stayed away from: the Deepwell except under Keth eyes, the Flats with a drawn blade, and any claim, Crown or House, on this water.

Taan put both palms flat on the wet stone. "Then it is water between us. Say it at the Hold the way I say it here."

The reeve asked leave to take one measure now, and filled his skin. Taan let the drip fall three times before she lifted her hands off the stone. "Two skins a day, at dusk, at the foot of your gate, in Keth hands and no others. The first goes up when the Hold has said the Low Well is a grave, out loud, at its own gate, where the yard can hear it. Not before." She worked a cord off her wrist, four knots close together and one apart. "Show this to whoever carries it. Lose it and they will not know you."

"One skin, reeve. I count it going and I count it at your gate."

"Then up. My steps, nobody talks."

## CORIN

The reeve had told him to hold in the shade. The Keth had gone down into the rock and the reeve after them, and the shade was empty, and the Flats lay open below with the Bone Field beyond and a Dreyer wreck in it with things glinting.

He went down on the quiet step, across the glass, into the bones. He took a dead man's ward out of the wreck, and a maul, and crept back out onto the Salt Road, where the reeve found him later with a Keth cord on his wrist.

"Corin. There you are. It is done: Taan gave terms." And: "You left the rock twice against my order; we will speak of that inside, not here. Now with me to the yard, blade home, and stand at my shoulder while I say it."

## ANSEL

The clerk stood in the ring with the coat in the sand and a thin dark blade held low, the way a man holds a pen. "Reeve Varrick. Dram Hollis, of the Carriers' book, lately of other books. Step in, or be known for stepping back." And: "You told me to go to hell this morning. I have been standing in it since noon, waiting for you. House Dreyer sends its regards, and I bring them. Step in."

Ansel did not step in. He spoke to the wall first. "Hear me, Scar Gate, every man on the wall and you, Captain Ruhl. By the Crown's writ and my own name: the Low Well is a Keth grave. Four of the Keth died in it, two of them children, poisoned by the Hold under House Dreyer's name, and those four dead are on my name now. The Keth have given terms and water comes to the foot of this gate at dusk, in Keth hands. And there in the ring stands the man Dreyer left in our walls, by his own mouth, before all of you."

"Corin. My blade and my ward, now, into my hands. Quick."

"Four dead in the Low Well. Two children. You say it well, Reeve; it is what the Crown bought you for." Hollis had not poisoned a well; he kept a book. "But yes: I am the man Dreyer left in your walls, by my own mouth, before all of them. Step in, Ansel Varrick."

Ruhl gave his ruling from the line: the ring held and he held it; answer or yield. To Corin: give the reeve his blade and his ward and stand clear.

Ansel made the clerk an offer instead: sheathe, walk out the Scar Gate before the shadow reached the road, and Scar Gate would not hunt him. "That is a good entry, Reeve. I would have written it myself. But you have read the wrong column." Dreyer did not pay a man for the road. "Step in."

He set his feet and brought his empty hands up. "Then you will have your answer, Hollis, and not a speech. But a reeve does not step on the line with empty hands for Dreyer's amusement. Corin Vass. Blade and ward, into my hands, now. The captain has given you the order twice over."

"Vass, the reeve asked you twice and I ordered it once. The ring waits for no man's slow hands."

Corin did not move.

## RUHL

He stood on the line and counted.

The first stroke came in low and fast, and the reeve's forearm was there before it; the edge found air. "Good," Hollis said. "You can stand. Nine days in the post and you can stand."

The second was a feint high and the real stroke low at the thigh, and the reeve was not where it went. "Twice. I will write that down later, if there is a later." The reeve answered the wall and not the blade: Dreyer's man had swung at an unarmed reeve and missed. "Unarmed by choice, Reeve. Write what you like. I am not here to be remembered."

The third went in flat under the lifted arm when the reeve turned his head to shout for Vass, a hand's width of dark steel along the ribs, and came out red. "One. That is the column I came to fill, Reeve. The rest were practice."

The fourth found nothing. "Bleed a while. I am in no hurry."

The fifth was the slow one, up under the ribs on the side already red, a quarter turn on the way out, and the reeve's knee dipped. "Two. You are wounded, Reeve. Yield to me, or finish it standing. Both are in your book."

"I yield the ring, Hollis. Write it in your column: the reeve stood in it unarmed, took two of yours, and yields to Dreyer's man by old law before the whole wall. The ring is yours. The Hold is not. Captain Ruhl, hear me: this man struck the Crown's reeve and House Varrick has outlawed him on the sand. When he steps off the line, the ring's law ends and the Hold's begins. Hold him for the Crown, alive, and nobody touches him before the Keth come at dusk. I am still reeve, and I am still breathing."

"Heard, Scar Gate. The reeve of the Crown's writ yielded in the ring to Dram Hollis, House Dreyer's man, before his captain and six on the wall. Nobody struck him after. Nobody will." And lower, to the man on his knee: "You chose the entry that lets you drink at dusk, Ansel Varrick. It was the right one."

Ruhl had it, every word. "Put that thin blade down on the sand and yield to me. Refuse and I take you with six men from the wall, and I will not make that gentle." And to the reeve: "Sit down before you fall."

Hollis said he would walk, slowly, to the Salt Road. "You do not walk, Carrier. Six on the wall have a bow on your back, and I am done talking."

The clerk did not turn to look at the wall. "Six bows and a captain. That is a sum even a reeve could add." He turned the thin blade hilt out and lowered it to the sand. "Here is Dreyer's blade, and here is Dreyer's man with it, and both of them are yours to keep for the Crown. I told you. Waiting is the only thing I am good at. I can do it in your cellar as well as in your yard."

As he went down, something fell out of the coat: a folded letter under a seal the colour of old blood.

"Taken, Hollis. Lay the blade on the sand and walk ahead of me into the Hold." The clerk laid the blade down, stooped, and picked up the paper. "Guild correspondence. Dull as dust." Ruhl took the blade off the sand himself. "Red wax, a well with a bar through it. Dreyer's sigil. That is the Crown's to read, not yours to burn."

## OSSA

Word came down to the Landing ahead of anyone: Varrick back in his yard with a Keth truce, and the clerk in the ring calling him. She held the casks. The Guild carried for whoever held the gate.

She looked up the Salt Road. It was empty, white and long, and nothing came down it. She went on looking.

## DRAM

Inside, where the Captain's law ran, there was no fire he could reach.

"Here, Carrier, in the Hold, where my law runs. The letter. Give it to the reeve with your own hand, or I take it from you with mine, and you know I would rather not."

The reeve asked for it unbroken. He asked his guard, again, for his blade and his ward. The guard said, "Hm."

Hollis looked at the paper in his inked fingers, and then at the reeve. "I was told to burn it. I am a clerk; I do not burn paper. That was always going to be the trouble with me." He held it out seal up. "From Dreyer's hand to yours, Ansel Varrick, by mine, under your captain's roof. Guild business. It was never dull."

The reeve broke the seal. H. The reeve in the ring, before the moon turns. Keep the Keth thirsty: no truce, whatever it costs. The Flats are ours when the Hold is empty. Burn this. D.

"Witness it, Captain, and the wall after you. Dreyer's seal, a well with a bar across it, and Dreyer's hand." It would go to the Crown with Hollis alive to say whose hand it was.

"Alive to say whose hand it is," Hollis said, as if entering it. "A clerk attests; that is the whole of the trade."

The reeve put the Captain on the wall with the bows down and himself alone at the foot of the gate with Taan's cord in his hand; Vass on the inner door; and Hollis to drink and wait.

"Drinks and waits. I have been ordered to do worse." He went to the cistern, took one draw, no more, and drank it standing, looking at the crack you could put two fingers in. "Eleven, Captain. I am counting the water. You may put it against my name."

Then he found the wall furthest from the door and sat down against it with his back straight and his hands on his knees, and waited for the light to go grey.
