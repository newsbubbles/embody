## Next: private property (Nate's thought, not built)

Locks make ownership something the world can see. A proposal: a lock or a place can name its `owner` (a person or a
faction). Picking or breaking an owned lock is theft: when it is heard (pick_failed, lock_broken) or found out later
(the owner finds it open), the owner remembers who, and their faction's standing with the thief drops. An owner in
their own room greets a stranger with a line, and a guard of that faction can turn them out, like the counting house
does now. Candidates: Ruhl's quarters in the Hold (his pay chest), Ossa's tally chest at the Landing, Taan's things at
the Deepwell (the old count), the Dry Camp's medicine chest.

## 1. Does the world lead to the end of chapter two?

The moon turns and the world counts: the truce, men on the Hold's wall, water in the Hold, who the convoy's water went
to, and whether the writ was handed over. Four contests feed that count: the drifters at the Old Salt Works, the
cistern (done), proof for the Keth, and Rook's convoy water. What the check found:

- **The end is a count, not a fight.** Dreyer's riders exist only as fires in a scene on day nine and as words at the
  moon. There is no place where they are, so nobody can go and meet them, scout them, rob them or foul their water.
  The players can only wait for the confrontation; nothing lets them bring it. (Proposal A fixes this.)
- **The proof sits unfound after eight days.** The empty phial (Low Well cairns) and the Dreyer manifest (Bone Field
  wreck) are both hidden with hints on their places, but no person in the world points anyone there. Taan wants proof
  and has said so; nobody says where it might be. (Fix: Taan, the Keth bearer and Mother Sallow each get one line that
  points at a place, not at the thing.)
- **The convoy goes two ways.** Chapter two's opening scene has the crawler driver say the convoy goes *south* at the
  moon; the Guild Yard's lore (and the route book, and the Master up the road) say the Guild carries *north*. South
  is Dreyer's dunes. (Fix: north everywhere, and one remembered line to Ossa so her mind says it right.)
- **The Guild Yard has no stake in the moon.** Sixty shards a step from the Landing, four armed men, a hauler, and
  none of it counts. (Proposal A gives the Guild a reason: one of theirs is held by the riders.)
- **The Hold's water is one cracked cistern.** Every water story so far is "carry it from somewhere". Nothing lets
  the Hold make water or claim a source of its own. (Proposals B and C.)
- **Every place is held by a faction already.** No ground is wild, so there is nothing to find that nobody owns and
  no reason to walk off the known road.

## 2. New ground

Each is a place or a few, with who holds it, its water, what is locked and what the prize is for, and what it feeds.

### A. The Riders' Hollow (House Dreyer, fills with the fires on day nine)

A long hollow in the south dunes beyond the Dry Camp, under a second rib of black rock. Until the fires it is
empty ground with old fire rings and picket stakes. On day nine Dreyer's riders ride in and it fills:

- **Captain Iven Strake**, Dreyer's sworn sword, and **six riders** with no minds (a routine: water, picket, a
  rider to the Dry Camp at dawn and dusk). Strake carries the paychest's key.
- **The riders' paychest**, locked (pick 17, break 19): the shards Dreyer buys drifters with. Empty it and Dreyer
  cannot buy a drifter for the rest of the chapter.
- **The water cart**: the riders' only water. Foul it, cut it or drive it off, and fewer riders come up at the moon.
- **Sel Amory**, hauler fourteen's driver, alive, tied in the picket line (Garran's friend, the strongbox code's
  name). Freeing him is the Guild crew's job, and the reason the yard cares who holds the gate.
- **Dreyer's plan for the gate**: a blueprint for glass caltrops to be laid at the Hold's gate the night of the
  moon. Stolen, it builds for the thief.

What it changes: the moon's count gains the riders. Riders who are thirsty, unpaid or short a captain are fewer, and
the Hold needs fewer men on the wall to turn them back. The players can bring the ending about. The reckoning gets
one more input; that is in the Reach's own scripts, mine.

### B. The Thornbrake and Tessel's Steading (wild)

A belt of glassthorn and barrel cactus in the Scar's lee south of the Hold, between the Gate Yard and the Bone Field.
A family farmed the Scar's runoff here before Dreyer's time and left.

- **Tessel's Steading**: a roofless house in the brake. A **cupboard** (closed, not locked) holds, wrapped in an old
  shirt, a blueprint for a **dew still**: glass panes and cloth over a pit, a draw or two of water every dawn, built
  with the works pack. A still in the Hold's yard counts toward the moon's water.
- **The root cellar**, under a trapdoor with a lock. Its key is on a cord around the neck of the last Tessel, whose
  bones lie in the brake (a hidden find). In the cellar: a full cask (8 draws), seed jars, and the steading's deed
  with a line in another hand: the well right taken "for the house's use" under Dreyer's seal. A second, smaller
  proof for Taan.
- **Water**: barrel cactus. Tap one for a draw; the brake holds three and they fill again in a week.
- **Danger**: **thornbacks**, boar-sized and plated in glass scale, out at dusk. A pack of three; down or drive off
  the big one and the other two scatter.

### C. Weeping Rock (wild, then whoever claims it)

A cleft in the Scar wall north of the Hold, off the Salt Road before the Crawler's Lee, where sweet water runs down
fern and moss into a basin. Six draws, two more each dawn. The Keth know it as the child's well and leave it alone
because of what nests there.

- **Danger**: **glass wasps**, a swarm in the cleft. A blade does nothing to a swarm. Smoke does: something lit, held
  or set burning in the cleft (the burn verb already makes fire) drives them off for a day.
- The water tastes like Cass's seep at the Salt Works: it comes from the west, under the Scar.
- Carved above the basin, worn soft: a crown over a closed eye. The Host has drunk here.
- Whoever holds Weeping Rock at the moon has water the riders do not count on.

### D. The Host's Lamp (hidden, for later)

On top of the Scar above Weeping Rock (a hard climb, 15), where Marr's drifters see a light some nights: a stone hut
with a Crown lock on its hatch. The lock opens to the Crown flask from Red Ridge (the flask is the key). Inside: Host
stores (two Crown skins, a signal lamp) and a blueprint for a **Crown signal lamp**. Your call: does the first torn
page of the Hold's daybook lie here? If yes, the seal quest can start in chapter two; if no, the Lamp waits.

### E. The Glass Teeth (wreckers, for later)

North past the Salt Works, the road runs through a broken pass of glass spires where wreckers, drifters gone bad,
jump haulers. Work for Hauler Nine and Binx's four once the moon is done.

## 3. Beasts and fights

- **Thornbacks** (B): a pack. Down the leader and the rest run.
- **Glass wasps** (C): a swarm. No blade or ward stops it; smoke or flight does. Stand in it and lose a point of
  vigor a minute.
- **Sandhounds**: lean dogs of the Flats that come at night to a body that is down or bleeding. A body down on the
  Flats becomes an emergency, not a nap.
- **Brine leeches** at the Salt Works seep: drink unstrained and take a wound.
- **The Thresher** stays as it is.
- **Riders** (A): men, so the d20 rules as they are, but six at once. Night raids on the cart and the paychest are
  stealth against their watch, not a brawl.

Beasts are bodies with the d20 stats and a behavior. The swarm and the pack's nerve are small scripts in the Reach,
not engine work.

## 4. Stacks: Emberglass x60

Half there already. The works pack counts a thing with `count` in its state as that many units. The village verbs do
not: take, give, put and drop move a whole thing. The engine change is small: those four verbs take a `count`
param, split a stack when you take part of it, and merge what arrives into a stack of the same name and kind already
there. Bulk is per unit. The engine session owns that; the Reach then changes its own scripts that count shards
(drifter pay, Rook's price) and folds the strongbox's sixty shards into one stack at boot.
