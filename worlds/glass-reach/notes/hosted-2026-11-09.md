# The Glass Reach, hosted (2026-11-09)

The first hosted Reach went up on Railway today. This note is the record of where it lives and how it is tended; per-player notes go beside it as the testers come in.

## Where

- Railway project `embody`, service `glass-reach`, environment `production`, in Nate's personal workspace.
- Public door: `https://glass-reach-production.up.railway.app/mcp` (bearer grants only). `GET /health` answers without one.
- The database is on the volume `glass-reach-volume` mounted at `/data` (50 GB, SQLite). The image holds the engine, the packs and `worlds/` from this repository at `/app`; `EMBODY_WORKSPACE=/app` tells the engine where the worlds are since the database is not beside them.
- Deploys: `railway up` from the repository root (the folder is linked). The boot renews the world with `load --upgrade --hosted`: new code, same state, timers left where they are. A redeploy is a few seconds of downtime; grants and seats survive it.

## What is true on the first boot

- 42 entities, 16 schemas, six playable seats (the reeve, Dram, Ruhl, Ossa, Sefa, Nico), four templates (carrier, guard, keth, dreyer).
- Clock at rate 4, paused. It runs while any grant holder is in a body and pauses when the last one leaves. (Since the night of 2026-11-09: it runs while a player has acted or spoken in the last quarter hour; held seats, waiting players and minds do not keep it running. The first night with watchers kept every seat alive and the clock ran until the cistern cracked with nobody at the keyboard.) The spine is in world seconds from the first join: heat at 21600 (every day), Dram's move at 28800 (once), dusk at 43200 and night at 54000 (every day), the cistern's crack at 64800 (once), dawn at 86400 (every day). A game day is six real hours of occupied time.
- Grants at the end of the day: an owner grant for Nate (his agent invites, lists and revokes over the door, and the viewer is his), the reeve's seat (Nate plays the reeve himself; the grant was made as "first tester" and kept), a player grant on the guard template for his friend, with a brief from the host: Captain Ruhl has sent him to find the reeve and stand with him. An earlier author grant for Nate is still live and unused; the owner grant replaces it.

## The viewer

The same deploy put the live graph at `https://glass-reach-production.up.railway.app/watch`. The page is public and empty; its data answers to owner, author, narrator and observer tokens only, and shows that grant's world. Paste the token once and the tab keeps it; the invite's viewer line also gives a `/watch#token=...` link that does the same without showing the token in the address bar. Checked on deploy day: the page answers 200, the data 401 without a token, 200 with Nate's; a player token gets a 403 that names the roles.

## Tending it

Invites are now a sentence to Nate's agent on its owner grant: `invite("player", entity="Sefa of the Keth", label="...")`, or `invite("player", template="guard", label="...", brief="...")` for a new character with orders of their own; `grants()`, `revoke(id)`, `seats()`, `clock()` the same way. The paste the server returns is a link and one line; the friend drops the link into their agent, which reads the page behind it (token, add-server lines, how to begin, how to tell it) and opens in the world. The link is good for a day and until the token's first use.

The same works from the command line inside the container, where the hosting process itself makes the grants (and is the only one that may make an owner grant):

    railway ssh -- embody --author --world glass-reach invite player --entity "Sefa of the Keth" --label "<name>"
    railway ssh -- embody --author --world glass-reach invite player --template guard --label "<name>" --brief "<their orders>"
    railway ssh -- embody --author --world glass-reach grants
    railway ssh -- embody --author --world glass-reach seats
    railway ssh -- embody --author --world glass-reach clock
    railway ssh -- embody --author --world glass-reach revoke <grant id>

`railway ssh` needs an SSH key registered on the Railway account and `ssh.railway.com` in `known_hosts`. `railway logs` is the server's log; `railway logs --build` the image build.

The token in an invite is shown once. Pastes are kept outside this repository, never committed, never in chat logs.

## The owner test

Nate's agent, on the owner grant from a session of its own (2026-11-09 evening): orientation, a throwaway observer invite and its revoke worked; the owner-only ceiling held (`invite("owner")` over the door is refused with "only the host's own process makes owner grants"). Three fixes came out of it the same evening: `last_used` was never written (now once a minute per grant, from the door), `restore` and `load_world` over the door took any path on the server's disk (now inside the workspace only), and nothing told a guest its own role (`use()` now answers `you` with principal, role and grant, and `principals()` over the door answers for the caller). Still open from the review: no way to relabel a grant or change its expiry, and no token fingerprint to match a paste file to a grant id.

## Leaving word

Nate's ask, playing the reeve while a friend was on his way in on the guard template: the olden-days message. `leave_word` on every person of the Reach (`act("leave_word", "Captain Ruhl", {"for": "the new guard", "word": "I went north to the Landing. Follow when you can."})`). Ruhl keeps it in his secret `word_left` with the place and the hour, and whispers it (reach 0, so Dram in the yard hears nothing) to the first one the word fits who talks to him, once. `for` matches a name, the template ("guard"), the type or the faction, or "anyone". Dram asking "where is the reeve" hears "Reeve Ansel Varrick left word here. Not for you." The table listener and the word listener are separate, so a mind embodying Ruhl keeps receiving the words (the keep listener always runs) and answers in its own voice. The upgrade adds `word_left` to every person's secret list, so `load_world(upgrade=true)` on the hosted Reach brings it in without touching anyone's state.

## The dry day, and what a player could read

The first evening on Railway, Nate asked why the viewer feed moved once a second with nobody at the gate and whether everyone would be parched by the time he came back. Read with the owner grant: the clock was running because two guests were in (his own reeve session and the friend's guard, Corin Vass), and three rows a second were `tick` events from thirst, quench and the fighters. Captain Ruhl, Nico and Sefa were at water 0 and vigor far below zero, down in the yard and on the ridge: thirst was tuned for the turn-based story (one point a minute) and never rescaled for the 86400 s day, nobody walked to water, vigor had no floor, and a body that is down did not drink. The same evening the friend's agent wrote back that `inspect` on the world root showed it the whole plan, policy and template briefs included, and that it kept it out of Corin's head.

What changed, in the engine and the pack: ticks write no rows; the world has a `pace` (the Reach: thirst every 8640 s, quench every 600 s, fights keep 6 s), set in `host()` so the upgrade re-times the standing ticks; thirst takes vigor down to 0 and no further and marks `downed_by: thirst`; a thirsty body with nothing here walks one known step to water, drinks, fills its skin and walks back to its post, quietly, so the story takes no first sight from it; a body that is down drinks what is put in its hand and gets up if thirst put it down (so Corin's plan, a filled skin for the Captain, works in the fiction); `policy` is never shown to a player and a template shows only its type, place and doc. The same friend's agent had been giving him bullet points: the player paste and the join briefing now ask for prose, in the fiction, no stat sheets.

After the deploy, the three who went down before the fix are restored by hand from the owner grant (water 10, vigor to max, down and wounded cleared); the rules would only get them up once someone put a skin in their hands.

## Known edges

- Railway refuses a Docker `VOLUME` line; the Dockerfile only makes the folder and the platform mounts over it.
- `railway.json` config-as-code is deprecated by Railway after 2026-12-01 in favor of `.railway/railway.ts`; migrate before then (`railway config migrate`).
- Live authoring through an author grant persists in the database, but the file-side record of those changes lands on the container's own disk and is gone on redeploy. Author from the repository and deploy.
- Unheld NPCs run on their behaviors only. A talking NPC needs a `mind` grant held by someone's subagent.

## The first friend's game

Corin's player came in on a link invite the evening the Reach went up and played until he went to bed. His word at the end: "it's good". What he named: the world held together and followed its rules, which earlier rounds never got right, and his agent never invented a fact, not even while the world was broken. During the dry day every NPC lay down from thirst and every skin was empty; his agent read the state honestly, told him so in the fiction, and could give him the rate the bodies were drying at (10 water to 0 in about two and a half real minutes). Those notes are what the fix was built from, and the redeploy that carried it went out with no downtime he noticed.

He built his own interface. [embody-viewer](https://github.com/LK-Studios/embody-viewer) (LK Studios) is the first interface a player's agent made for a hosted world: it reads the Reach from inside his seat, live, with a simple low-fi animated scene (halftone or ascii, a narrator panel, his wounds on screen). His other MCP servers, a lorekeeper and a Higgsfield one, turned moments into stills and a 30-second clip with the exact lines from the scene: him walking the salt road alone during the dry day, Corin kneeling by Captain Ruhl laid out in the yard unable to make him drink, the four of them on Red Ridge with the Keth scouts. The images agree with the log: who stood where, who held what, the long glass. The engine kept the world; his tools showed it. That is the bet: make the world good, and the interfaces come from the players' own agents. His images are kept outside this repository; the viewer is public and credited in the README.

What it taught the engine, the same night: seats lapsed too soon (a quarter hour, now an hour); minds looping on `wait` went mute when their sessions ended a turn, so `wait(until=...)` and `embody watch` replace the loop; and the friend's seat held the reeve's blade and ward after he went to sleep, which left the reeve barehanded in the duel. A seat that sleeps with someone else's things is a story the engine should let the owner end in one call; the owner session's evict was refused by its own harness, so for now it is Nate's hand.
