# Author: building or repairing a world

Read this before you make or change a world: schemas, scripts, rules, timers, the world folder.

Order: `create_world` > `define_script` > `define_schema` > `spawn` places > `connect` them > spawn contents > `layout` the map > `set_rule(root, ...)` for the storyline > `schedule` timers > `clock(turn=3)` > play it yourself once.

- Read `guide('scripting')` first; it is the full ctx reference.
- Holder verbs carry `targets` (a type) and a `scope` (`exits`, `here`, `held`, `any`) so players see only possible pairs. Object verbs do not. Give slow verbs a `duration`.
- Rules are listeners on a place or the root with `kinds=["before_act"]`; `ctx.reject(reason, hint)` is how they say no, in the world's words. A rule installed with `set_rule` hears every event under its entity whatever the reach, so a root rule is the table's rule; schema listeners hear only what bubbles past them. A holder verb with `self: true` may also target the actor (`wake`, `check`).
- Ambient facts: an `ambient` dict on the root (`time_of_day`, `weather`, `light`) and one on any place that differs; perceive shows them merged. Drive them with timers (`schedule`, `timers`, `unschedule`) and a root rule that patches them and emits `scene` events.
- A storyline is a rule on the root plus a hidden entity for its facts. Advance on events (`arrived`, `talked`, your own `emit` kinds) and check facts, not a stage number, so play off the rails still finds the ending. Emit `scene` events at a place to narrate to whoever stands there.
- People: `dialog` keyed by words (`"ale|drink|beer"`), entries a line or `{"line", "after_gift", "if_state": {...}, "else"}`; `gives` for what a topic hands over; all three secret. The listener that answers carries `unless_embodied: true` so a mind can take over the mouth without losing the table.
- `brief` on an entity is your instruction to whoever embodies it: voice, wants, what it knows, how it judges people. Always secret; a mind reads it in its own card. Write one only on the people who deserve a mind: the narrator reads a brief as your mark to give them one when the player engages them. `memory` is theirs, you only read it. Seed a character's goals with `intend(text, why, entity=...)`; the mind manages them from there.
- Anything a player or mind could meet comes in through `introduce`, not a bare `spawn`, and you answer four questions in the call. Where: the place it is in. How it came (`how`): `always_there` (it has been here all along: no sound, nobody saw it appear), `arrived`, `delivered` or `built` (`by=`), or `appeared` when the game says things do; every way but `always_there` needs the `scene` the room sees. Who already knows it (`known_by` {entity: what they know}): a hidden thing with one keeper, a plain thing everyone nearby would know, a stranger nobody knows (`{}`). What a new person knows (`remembers`): the road they came by, their errand, who sent them. Something sent for, on its way, takes `after=` (world seconds): `known_by` hear the news now (write it as news), and the thing and its scene land when it comes; `unschedule` calls it off. A bare `spawn` is for the build, before anyone plays: in a running world the room hears a thing appear from nowhere, and the minds there say so.
- A thing an NPC hands over should exist: put it in their inventory and name it in `gives` as `{"item": <name>}`, so the table and an embodied mind both give the same real object with `give`.
- Close the loops. For every number that falls (water, hp, a cask's `left`, ammunition, standing), name what raises it; for every source of that, name what refills the source; follow the chain until it ends in something the world makes on its own (a well, a dawn crawler, rest) or in something rare on purpose. Water in the Reach: thirst takes it, `drink` gives it, `fill` fills the skin from a cask, the Keth run fills the jars at dusk, the Deepwell never runs dry. Health had no way back at all until `mend` and `tend`: a world with a stat that only falls runs down to it, slowly, and nobody notices until a body lies on the road for good.
- The rates are the design. A refill as fast as the drain makes the stat background (water beside a well); slower makes it scarce and gives the day its shape (water carried up at dusk, a medic with four doses); a refill only a rare thing gives makes a quest, and whoever holds that thing has power. Write one side and ask what the other is: the answer is often a verb (`tend`), a role (a medic, a bearer), a route (a crawler) or a trade, and that is where play comes from. Leave a loop open only on purpose, and let the story say so (the cistern cracks; nothing refills it). Before you host, run the world with nobody playing (`python packs/d20/soak.py <world> --days N` for water) and read what ran out and what never moved.
- Lay it out once the tree stands, in one `layout(frame=..., placements=[...], paths=[...])` pass from the root down: the frame on the root (metres, z up, y north, optional `bounds`), each place with `at` and one extent (`radius`, `size` [w, d, h] or a `footprint` polygon; `floor` and `ceiling` for a cave or a cellar), a `route` on each connects_to edge, then the things a viewer would draw inside each place. The tree is still the story: a layout says where a place lies and how big it is, never what holds what, and siblings may overlap (a landing on the flats' edge). Take the numbers from the canon (travel times, the briefs' words), then read the map back with `map(sketch=true)` and `measure(a, b)` and fix every warning. What has no `at` of its own is where its container is. A `mesh` is a reference to a model file, never assumed; a grid inside a room maps into the frame by its `space.frame_origin` and `cell`. In `build.py`, write each piece only where it has none yet, so the owner's live edits survive a reboot.
- Sheets (the sheet pack, after d20 in `requires`): a body with a `sheet` rolls d20 + an attribute modifier + a skill rank + its grants, every other body body/wits/will + a skill as before; d20's `bonus(who, skill, ctx)` is the one place both are read. Write a whole sheet with `patch(body, {"sheet": {"attributes": {...}, "skills": {...}}}, replace=true)` (a plain patch merges into the old one); `to_sheet(state)` in packs/sheet/pack.py converts a d20 body so it rolls what it rolled before. Add a skill by patching the Skillbook's `skills` ({name: {primary, secondary, scope, related}}); tune every number in the root's `sheet_rules`. `grants` on a body are bonuses with a source, skill, place, until and uses. A world verb with a dc of its own teaches with `learn(ctx, who, skill, dc, success, against)`; a weapon names its skill with `skill`. Rules that read `wits` or `body` raw still read the old stats, so a sheet body keeps them. guide('skills') shows the skillbook.
- Positions: everything on the map resolves to a spot by one rule. Its own `at`; else its grid cell in its container's `space`; else wherever its container is (a thing carried rides on its carrier, a thing in a chest is where the chest is). `map` and `measure` say which (`how`: own, grid, inherited, with `from`). Every place, and anything standing directly in a place, needs a spot of its own; the place's centre is not one. Name each place's spots once with `landmarks` on its layout (`{"the cistern": {"at": [x, y, z], "radius": 3}}`), the spots people would say, then place by name: `at="the cistern"` on `spawn`, `introduce` and `move` and in a `layout` placement, or the name of a thing there for a spot beside it. A thing with an extent is solid (nobody stands in the cistern) unless its layout says `solid: false`. Each reply that leaves something in a place with no spot carries `position_notice` with the fix. `verify(under=...)` audits a subtree, a page at a time: what has no spot, what stands outside its container, what stands inside something solid, each with a fix; `apply=true` takes the suggested spots for that page, though a spot chosen by landmark reads better on the map. A thing put down lies where its holder stood, and a body that walks in stands where the drawn way comes in, so draw the paths. Positions change no play: no reach, range or cover.
- Edits mid-play are the job, not a failure: `patch` merges (`{"dialog": {"weather": "..."}}` adds one topic), `replace=true` sets whole, `None` deletes. Redefine a script and the next act uses it. `rename(entity, name)` gives someone a new name (a guest who joined under a handle takes a character name): the room hears it, the old name stays in the log, and a name someone else has is refused.
- Build what the speech names. Read what the NPCs have said against what exists: a place, person or thing named by three or more speakers with nothing behind it (a well in every Keth line, the nine men the captain counts water for) is the next thing to build. Keep descriptions to what does not move: a blade at the hip goes stale the day it is handed over, while the card's `bears` list follows the blade. A rack, shelf or chest is a holder: `holds` lists what it takes (`["weapon"]`, `["any"]`), `room` how many, and the `put` verb (village) lays a thing on it where anyone there can take it.
- A mechanic the engine lacks (locks, vehicles, keys, a ferry that runs on a timetable) is built, never designed around: a pack (`packs/<name>/` with its schemas, verbs and tests) when other worlds will want it, or a scripted object in the world when only this one does. A strongroom with no lock is not "guarded by the story". Locks exist: `requires: ["locks"]`, then spawn a `lock` inside the box, door or place it keeps shut (`key`, secret `code`, `pick_dc`, `break_dc`); `pick` rolls d20 + wits + the best of the `lockpick`, `stealth` or `craft` skill, so give `lockpick` to those who have the trade; nothing in a locked box is in reach and `go` refuses a locked door or place. Stacks exist: give a thing a `count` (Emberglass, `count` 60, `bulk` 0.1) instead of spawning sixty; take, drop, give and put move part of one with `params {"count": n}`, and like stacks join where they land. Many copies of one made thing are one stack too, never N spawns: fourteen swords on a rack are one entity with `count` 14, and `take` with count 1 parts one off. A piece joins a stack only while its state is the stack's but for `count`, so one that was marked, booked or used stays a thing of its own when it is put back. Keep apart what a verb changes one at a time: a thing that destroys itself when used (a thrown charge) would take the whole stack with it, and a per-piece flag (`loaded`) would set on all of them. In scripts count with `ctx.count(holder, what)` and move with `ctx.transfer`. Sleep exists (d20: `sleep`, `wake`, `away(sleep=true)`): a sleeper's feed keeps only what is loud or done to it, so an alarm, a collapse or a bell a sleeper should hear is emitted with `loud=True` or a reach of 2. Water is spent by work too (d20 sweat): a blow, `harvest`, `dig`, `dress`, `break` and a heavy walk on open ground cost a point each, and a sleeper dries at half the pace; tune it with a `sweat` table on the root (`{"dress": 2, "fight": 0}`, or `false`), and give a new heavy verb its cost there. Vehicles exist: `requires: ["vehicles"]`, then spawn a `vehicle` (a place inside the place where it stands, seen there as a thing) with its `stops` (and a `route` through the places between), `leg_time`, `seats`, `cargo`; a `wheel` inside it drives it. People `board` and `alight`; `drive` sets it out along its road, and halfway through each leg it rolls into the next place, where anyone can see it, `halt` it, board it, or stop it with a thing that `blocks_road`. A `timetable` ({"every": s} or {"at": [seconds into the day]}) and a `driver` NPC run it with no mind; a world rule sends it with `dispatch` or keeps it in with `hold`. Fuel, breakdowns and a `hull` are off until you set them (packs/vehicles/pack.py has every key). Beasts exist: `requires: ["village", "d20", "beasts"]` and `install_rules(s, root, day="dawn")` from packs/beasts/pack.py, then a hidden `species` (stages by age, `eats`, `tame_dc`, `tame_at`, breeding, `yields`) and `beast` bodies naming it. Put `forage` on the places they live ({"root": {"left": 8, "max": 8, "regrow": 4}}): the regrow is how many the land carries, and a herd grows to it and stops. Food items carry `food` and `feeds`; people `feed` a beast from the hand and `gentle` it once a day until it is theirs; a kept beast follows its owner, needs feeding, and goes feral when it is not. Hunters too: a species with `preys_on` hunts when hungry (once a night with `install_rules(..., night="dusk")`), takes the weakest within reach, and a kill is a carcass of `meat` (then `carrion`, then bones) that beasts eat and people `butcher`; give a hunter `wanders_in` so it comes back once its prey is plentiful, and its prey keeps its last few out of reach. Ledgers exist: `requires: ["ledgers"]`, then extend `ledger` for a device (a slate, a book, a cord) and give it to someone: they `record`, `void` (nothing is erased), `read`, `search`, `total`, `verify` against the log, `copy` onto paper, `transfer` to a device here and `send` to a contact's, and nobody else ever reads it. Its `records` (event kinds) make it write down its holder's deeds by itself; `keeper` plus a lock inside binds it; `delivery` face or relay says how it sends (packs/ledgers/README.md). Unseen company exists: a body with `hidden: true` and a `shown_to` list is seen and heard only by those ids (a familiar at someone's side; a `reveal` verb adds to the list), `hands: false` keeps a body to its own verbs, and `ctx.ways(a, b)` measures a tether (docs/SCRIPTING.md).
- Log every change to a rule a seat plays by (a stat, a verb's cost, what kills, what a watch might wait on): `rules_changed(id, was, now, recheck=[...], guide=[...], retired=[...])` live, or `RULES = [...]` in the world's build.py (a pack keeps its own in pack.py), logged at the next build or upgrade. The world's rules version moves on, and every mind and guest behind it hears so in `perceive()` until it reads `guide('changes')`; minds that have played for hours otherwise act on the old rules. `was` and `now` are a sentence each; `retired` names every field and event kind that is gone, and a watch waiting on one is refused. The same id again edits that entry.
- Put a `policy` on the root: `fixed` (facts no narrator may bend), `fill` (may small props be improvised), `voice` (may NPCs speak beyond their tables).
- A world is a folder: `create_world(name, id=...)` makes `worlds/<id>/` with a `build.py` skeleton and starts a git repository in it. Live authoring lands there too (`define_script` writes `scripts/<id>.py`, the other author ops append to `changes.jsonl`), so name things rather than pass ids. Commit the folder as you go; versions, forks and backups of the code are git's, not the engine's.
- `build.py` carries `WORLD` (id, name, seed, description, the packs it `requires`), `define(s)` (schemas), `populate(s)` (entities, rules, clock) and `upgrade(s)` (what an older build lacks, each step checked so it runs twice without change); scripts are the files in `scripts/`, shared code comes in with `use("name")`. `load_world(id)` builds it, `load_world(id, upgrade=true)` renews a live world: new code, same state and history. `examples/ferry-at-dusk/` on the `village` pack is the template for a village of talk and chores; `worlds/glass-reach/` on the `d20` pack is the template for a world with stakes, factions, dice and a fight, where every rule of the game is a verb. `save(name)` and `restore(path)` carry a game as one file.
- A fresh game on the same state: `fork_world(source, new_id)`, then `use(new_id)` or `join(new_id, entity)`. The source stays as it was.
- After a playtest, read the notes, change the folder, bump the world with `upgrade`, commit, and tick the notes.
