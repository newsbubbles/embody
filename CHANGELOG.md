# Changelog

What changed in the engine, newest first: the core, the ops, the server and the packs. A world's own story lives in its folder (`worlds/<id>/notes/`) and its event log, not here. Every entry names what an agent or a host sees differently.

The version in `pyproject.toml` is 0.1.0 until the first release; until then entries go by date.

## Unreleased

- The maker's frame (docs/ACCESS.md, phase 2): a thing whose state has `maker` {types, scripts, caps, review, revoked} lets whoever holds it put verbs on the machines it names, through new ops `program`, `unprogram` and `programs` (players and minds; the owner and author without a frame) and `review` (owner and author). The grant follows the frame from hand to hand. The machine must be open to the holder (`w`). The code carries `under: "self"`: it changes the machine, its load and whoever chose the verb, never a rider; no spawn, no rules, default fuel; what it says is marked `maker` and reaches others as untrusted. Caps per frame (verbs, source size, verbs an hour); with `review` a verb waits for the owner; revoked, its verbs refuse. Verbs on one thing (`programmed` state) are new to the engine and show in perceive and inspect like schema verbs.
- Scripts get `ctx.rename(x, name)` (the `rename` op's rule: a name nobody else has).
- Access (docs/ACCESS.md): a thing may carry `access` {owner, group, mode, verbs}, an owner (a principal or a body), a group (members in `access_groups` on the root) and a mode like a file's (`rwxr-x---`). Its own verbs need `x`; a per-verb rule (`verbs`: {"drive": "u"}) covers any verb done to it, its owner's or anyone's. Without `r` another body's card of it has no state and no contents. On the authoring ops, every role but owner and author needs `w` to change it or to put something into it, and `w` also opens a thing outside a builder's plot. New ops `chmod` (rwxr-x---, 750, g+w; or per verb) and `chown` (owner, group), for the owner, the author and the thing's owner; what a builder or narrator spawns is its own. Scripts get `ctx.may`, `ctx.member`, `ctx.chmod` and `ctx.chown`. A thing with no access works as before.
- A limited grant (a builder) reads its own plot only: outside it, `inspect` shows the public card (no secret state, memory or brief), `perceive(from_entity=...)` and `recall(entity=...)` refuse and `find` leaves out hidden things, and its scripts read secret state as None. Access with `r` widens it.
- The `ledgers` pack (`packs/ledgers`, `requires: ["ledgers"]`; guide in its README): a `ledger` device people carry holds named ledgers that only its holder reads. `ledgers` (list, open one), `record`, `void` (a correcting entry; nothing is erased), `read` and `search` (filters on text, party, item, kind, tag, place, time or day, amount, source and voided; sort by time, amount, party, item or number; paged, with how many matched), `total` (grouped by item, party, ledger, kind or unit: count, in, out, net, running balance, min, max, mean, mode, standard deviation), `verify` (recorded entries checked against the log, hand entries unverifiable, copies by their first record), `copy` onto paper, `transfer` to a device in reach, `send` to a contact's device (`face`: both in one place; `relay`: anywhere, through a `ledger_relay` that logs who sent how many to whom, never what), `contacts`. `records` makes a device write its holder's deeds down by itself (given, taken...). `keeper` binds it, and a lock inside it is the binding's lock (pick or break it). Entries live on hidden pages in a hidden archive, so no card, room view, feed, chronicle or other body sees them, and the room hears one line the world sets; owners read the pages. A book of five thousand entries totals in about a second.
- Listeners: a schema listener with `holder: true` on a thing also hears its holder's deeds and what is done to them, wherever that happens (events whose actor, target or payload `by`, `who`, `to` or `source` is the thing's parent). `before_act` only when its `kinds` name it; a `stop()` up the tree does not silence it. Scripts get `ctx.event_at(seq)`, one event of the log (notes and intents excepted; a builder's script reads only events done to its own plot). An action may ask for more fuel with `fuel` (at most a million; the default stays 50k) for a verb that walks a long list. A hidden thing spawned in someone's sight no longer shows in their feed as `spawned`.
- The `vehicles` pack (`packs/vehicles`, `requires: ["vehicles"]`): things people ride that take time on the road. A `vehicle` is a place standing inside a place: seen there as a thing with its card, its riders and cargo inside and going where it goes. `board` and `alight` (also `arrived`, so world rules see people come and go), `drive` from aboard or on a `wheel` (params `to`: a place on its road, its `stops` and `route`; `direct`, `loop`, `offroad`), `halt` (alongside, or the one driving; `halt_dc` for one that does not stop for everyone), `repair`, `strike` (a `hull`), `refuel`. A trip has a middle: halfway through each leg it rolls into the next place (`passing`, `approaching`), where people see it, board it, halt it, alight into the road or block it (a thing with `blocks_road`), and it pulls in at the end (`pulled_in`); `departed`, `stopped`, `broke_down`, `struck`, `boarded`, `alighted` and a scene for each step, all on the vehicle at reach 1, so riders and the place both see them. Runs need no mind: a `timetable` (`every`, or times of the day) and an optional `driver` NPC, who must be aboard and on their feet; `dispatch` and `hold` let a world rule send it or keep it in, and a stopped run takes up its trip again once nothing stops it. Fuel, breakdowns and a hull are off until a world sets them. The trip's clock lives in the vehicle's state, so a trip survives a restart. The chronicle counts passing and upkeep as routine and a vehicle stopped, broken or struck as news.

- The chronicle reads like news. `chronicle(day)` leads with `news`, each item with `t` and `place`: people new to the world (spawned, or embodied for the first time, with faction and template), places spawned or first visited, fights one line a pair and place (blows, hits, damage), finds, downs, deaths, outlawings and the other notable kinds, gifts and trades between different sides, standing changes, and the story's state (the root, an entity named Story, and the meta's `chronicle_story`) as each key's first value that day against its last. The hint asks for player-facing news: what changed first, routine in a clause. `/chronicle.json` tells each scene text once.

Talk, look, wait, `pick` and `drink` stay free, and works labour pays in its own tick as before. - Sleep. d20 `sleep` lies a body down (`asleep` on its card), and `away(sleep=true)` does it as a seat goes away. Asleep it mends two points a beat for the water of one, and its feed keeps only what is done to it and what is loud (payload `loud`, or reach 2 and more: a lock broken, the thresher). It is easy prey: a blow rolls twice and keeps the better, against bare hands and no brace (`rolls` and `asleep` on `hit` and `missed`), and `search` works on a sleeper by stealth (d20 + wits + stealth against 10 + their wits: `lifted`, named on the thief, and the sleeper sleeps on; or `caught`, nothing taken, and they wake). A blow, a word said to them, `wake` by someone here, a loud event where they lie, thirst at 3 or their own next deed wakes them (`woke`, with `by` and `why`). The engine keeps the feed and the waking, so a world without d20 gets them by emitting `slept` and patching `asleep`.

- Stacks. A thing whose state has a `count` is that many of itself, and its card (and any list that names it) shows the `count`. village `take`, `drop`, `give` and `put` take `params {"count": n}`: part of a stack moves and the rest stays; with no count the whole stack moves, as before. What arrives joins a like stack (same type, name and state but for count; who handed it over does not matter), so sixty shards given one at a time are one Emberglass of 60. `bulk` is a unit's and may be a fraction (0.1 a shard). Events carry `count`: `taken`, `dropped`, `given`, `put`, `burned`, and `searched` (per item). d20 `search` takes part of a stack when only part fits; `harvest` puts each shard on the Emberglass stack in hand (`count` 1, then joined); works `spend` hands over part of a stack the same way. Scripts: `ctx.count(x)` and `ctx.count(holder, what)`, `ctx.transfer(x, to, count)`, `ctx.split`, `ctx.merge`, `ctx.bulk`, `ctx.load` (docs/SCRIPTING.md, Stacks).
- A deed's target named by a name several things share (two stacks of Emberglass, two waterskins) goes to the one in your reach that the verb would take, else the first in your reach, so its refusal says why. Before, `act("take", "Emberglass")` was refused as ambiguous whenever another Emberglass existed anywhere in the world.
- The `locks` pack (`packs/locks`): a `lock` is a thing spawned inside the box, door or place it keeps shut, with a `key` (a thing's name or id), a secret `code`, a `pick_dc` and a `break_dc`. `unlock` and `lock` take the key in hand or the code (a wrong code is refused and spends nothing; a lock with neither is a bolt anyone turns); `pick` (ten minutes, d20 + wits + the better of stealth or craft, rolled only if you stay) and `break` (two minutes, d20 + body, loud, broken for good). Events on what it locks: `unlocked`, `locked`, `lock_picked`, `pick_failed`, `lock_broken`, `break_failed`, with by, lock and holder.
- The engine knows a lock. Nothing in a locked holder (its own `locked`, or an `attached` child whose state says `locked`) is in reach of any verb, and the refusal says what opens it; the holder's card shows `locked` and its lock, never what is inside. A thing whose state has `attached` is in reach wherever its parent is (a lock on a door from both sides, a lock on a place from outside) and is listed in affordances. Exits show `locked` for a locked door or a locked place. Scripts ask `ctx.locked(x)`. village `go` refuses a locked door or place and `put` a locked holder, with the way to open it.
- `rename(entity, name)`: an author op (MCP tool, `embody op rename`, narrators too) that gives anyone or anything a new name, so a guest who joined under a handle can take a character name. Refused when someone else in the world already has the name, in any case (the rule `join` keeps). The room hears a `renamed` event {old, new}, the old name stays in the log, and the op is recorded and replayed like `patch`. A canon entity is the owner's to rename.
- village `burn`: a `burnable` thing, held or here, burned at a fire (a place or thing with `fire` or `lit`, or a lit light in hand). It is destroyed, what it held drops, and the room sees a `burned` event and a scene, never what it said. Nothing is taken out of memories or past `read` events: whoever read a letter keeps it. A world binds the verb on its people, with a few seconds' duration.
- d20 `search` honours `carry`: it takes what fits in the searcher's hands, by the same bulk count `take` uses, and leaves the rest on the body. The `searched` event names what was `left` and the log says so; a searcher whose hands are already full is refused before the search starts. Before, a robber walked off with everything a downed body held, past any limit.
- d20 `quench`: a thirsty body nobody plays walks to the nearest water it knows the way to, a step a look and up to six steps, not only one step; there it drinks its fill (to within a draw of full, the skin kept for the road) before it walks back to its post the same way. - The `works` pack (`packs/works`, design in docs/WORKS.md): things people build, in time. A `blueprint` is a thing in the world: portable (whoever holds it builds it where they stand, if the place fits its `where`; a stolen plan builds for the thief) or site-bound (only its patrons start it, there). `build` raises a `project` at the site that holds the plan, the materials and the pay. Each stage wants its materials in the works, its roles on hand and its hours of labour, and may settle for a while, when `undo` can pull it down (guards make that harder). The crew comes three ways: `hire` (paid from the works each pay day, two days unpaid and they walk off), `assign` (a faction's `leader` over that side's people with no mind) and volunteering (anyone takes an open role by `work`, an hour at a time). The crew nobody plays walks in during working hours, works and gets thirsty; take the plan out and the next stage waits for it. The finished thing is born with provenance `built`, and its hands, its patron and everyone there remember it. `survey` says what stops it, with what to do.
